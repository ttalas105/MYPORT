# Product

## Current Direction — Thomas Talas

Thomas’s portfolio is one continuous, scroll-driven Three.js visit to a late-night music and coding studio. The visitor starts across a nighttime city street, approaches the solid fitted walnut door, opens it through scrolling, crosses the threshold, and looks around at eye level before approaching LLM Driven Video Generator and continuing through the other project workstations and equipment. Neighboring buildings and a deep night sky frame the studio's sidewalk, brick parapet, roof and parked bicycle. The closed door sits flush at zero rotation with a jamb, seals and threshold. Warm lamps, dark wood, woven acoustic treatment and restrained violet light carry the atmosphere, with a quiet interface around them.

Project introductions are minimal semantic HTML synchronized to the camera, with contribution context below each heading. An “Explore project” button at every project station opens a longer reading view and approaches the current object with the camera. Native scrolling controls the guided route; the Enter action takes the visitor inside and the fixed Index keeps every project directly reachable. Studio music is enabled by default unless previously muted. The header Music button opens compact settings with a play checkbox, volume slider, track information and source link; music controls live only in this popover. The local soundtrack sounds muffled outside the door and clears as the visitor enters. Browser playback restrictions can require a first interaction. The studio is the primary experience, with its content and navigation available independently of WebGL.

The direction follows Thomas’s chosen guided walkthrough of a realistic studio and his interest in music and J. Cole. LLM Driven Video Generator, OKRA, Portal and Tapi occupy believable working surfaces in the same space. The room and furniture are modeled as a complete scene. Local CC0 maps distinguish oak floorboards, walnut-veneer furniture, plaster and woven fabric. Warm lighting follows the physical lamps, and reflective surfaces pick up the furnished room through a one-time desktop capture. Mobile retains the HDR environment; no photographic room background stands in for architecture.
